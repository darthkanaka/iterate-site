What an AI receptionist is, and what it takes to run one

What an AI receptionist does, where it works best, how it compares to an answering service, and what it frees your team to do.

By Kawika LopezOctober 12, 20268 min read

An AI receptionist is software that answers your business phone for you and talks with callers in ordinary sentences, the way a person at the front desk would. It works out what the caller needs, answers questions about your business, books a time or takes a name and number, and hands the rest to your team.

If you run a small business in Hawaiʻi, you've probably heard the term lately. Here's what it's good for. Calls get missed, and the ones that do get answered don't always end with the right details written down. An AI receptionist doesn't get tired, doesn't forget what to ask, and is never too busy with another caller to pick up.

It's at its best when it sticks to a few tasks it can do the same way every time, instead of trying to replace the job of an entire employee. The goal is to lighten your team's load where it's safe to, so your people have time for the work that needs a person.

We've designed dozens of AI voice agents and characters, and every one taught us how a voice conversation should feel, what AI does well on a call, and what the real-world limitations are.

What does an AI receptionist actually do on a call?

An AI receptionist handles the routine part of a call, from hello to handoff. It finds out why the person's calling and does the job you gave it, like answering questions, booking a meeting into an open slot on your calendar, taking a name and callback number, or passing the call to a person. When the call ends, it writes up what happened, and those notes go where they need to without anyone retyping them.

One we built answers the main line for a creative technology company here in Honolulu. It exists because calls came in while the team was in meetings or out of the office, and every new lead meant back and forth to find a time to meet. It's answered their phone full time since March 2026 and books their discovery meetings. After each call, the meeting goes onto their Google Calendar by itself.

The phone demo does a narrower job on purpose. It answers after hours for Island Emergency Restoration, a company we made up, and decides whether each call is an emergency. If it is, it takes a name and callback number and alerts the on-call tech, and you'll see the alert pop up on a phone drawn on the page. If not, it gives office hours.

How is it different from an answering service or a phone menu?

With an AI receptionist, callers explain things in their own words, and it acts on what they say, like putting a meeting on the calendar or alerting whoever's on call. An answering service is live people picking up for you. A phone menu, also called a phone tree, is a recording that sends callers down branches when they press keys.

We've worked with several businesses that were using answering services, and the same problems kept coming up. One answering service takes calls for dozens or even hundreds of businesses, each with its own rules. For the businesses we worked with, that meant calls came back missing the information they needed far more often than not.

Staff turnover made it worse, because the call instructions kept getting relearned. From the customer's side, it felt like every call started with "Sorry, I'm new and still figuring out how things work here." An AI receptionist learns your way of doing things once and asks every caller for the details you need, the same way every time.

What can't an AI receptionist do?

A good AI receptionist knows its limits, and that's what makes callers trust it. It can't know what you haven't told it, so it doesn't guess, and made-up prices or policies are one of the first things we test for. If a caller thinks it's an emergency, it takes them at their word. For anything outside its job, it says so plainly and passes the caller to a person.

Our demo stays in its lane on purpose. It gives no repair advice, no prices and no arrival times, and if a caller mentions active fire, smoke they're breathing, a gas smell, sparking electricity, or anyone hurt, it tells them to get everyone out and call 911, then ends the call.

Is an AI receptionist right for a small business?

An AI receptionist works best when it has a small, clear job it can do the same way on every call. It's especially good at answering calls after hours, when nobody else can, and at doing one front-desk task over and over, like booking a meeting. It's also great at answering quick questions like your hours or directions, then sending the calls that need a person to the right one.

Think of businesses that take calls around the clock, like emergency repair and damage mitigation, where the call at 2 AM is the one that matters most, or trades whose calls come in while the crew is on a job. It also fits an office where calls come in during meetings, like the creative technology company's main line, or a shop that hears the same few questions all day. It's a weaker fit when most calls need a judgment only you can make, like a quote that depends on seeing the job.

What does an AI receptionist free your team to do?

In Hawaiʻi, customer service is everything. An AI receptionist takes the repetitive, low-pressure parts of a call off your team, answering basic questions, taking down the details and handing them off without dropping the ball. That frees your people for the work that builds trust, like following up, giving updates, reassuring a worried customer and handling anything complicated. The AI catches the call, and your people build the relationship.

This is home for us, and we've been building these long enough to see the pattern. Some companies pitch an AI receptionist as a full replacement for your phones. That doesn't work here, because people choose a business by how it treats them. We could build a receptionist that does much more, but it's not what businesses end up wanting, because it's not what their customers need.

That's how we build them. The Honolulu receptionist books a meeting with the team, and the after-hours line alerts the on-call tech to call back. What does it take to run one?

Running an AI receptionist well takes more than the online do-it-yourself tools make it look, and that's the rabbit hole we keep owners out of. Getting one good conversation is the easy part. Getting it to handle every caller the same way, fit the tools you already use, and keep up as your business changes is where the hours go. When we build one, that's our job, not yours.

It also fits the way you already work. We connect it to the tools your team uses, like Google Calendar for bookings, text messages for alerts and confirmations, Slack for your team, and Salesforce for your customer records, so the details end up where your people will see them.

Should you build one yourself?

Only if you have someone on your team, or a whole department, with both the technical know-how and a real feel for customer service. Without that, it turns into a second job. Every business we've worked with tried building it themselves first. By the time they called us, they'd spent weeks or months figuring out how to get what they wanted.

That's time spent learning a whole new industry instead of running your own business. We've been down these rabbit holes across dozens of voice agents, so you can skip straight to one that works and keep your focus where it belongs. Here's what we build.

What else do owners ask?

Will my callers know it's an AI? Yes. The agent in our phone demo says it's an AI in its greeting, and if a caller asks whether it's a robot, it says yes. We'd set yours up the same way.

Does it sound like a robot? Talk to the phone demo and hear it for yourself.

Is it the same as a chat agent on a website? Not quite. People expect a short wait in a chat window, so a chat agent can take on more before it answers, but on a call they expect an answer as quick as a person's, so a voice receptionist usually does a smaller job to stay fast. Our demo's chat replies took 2 to 6 seconds, which feels normal in a chat and would feel like dead air on a call. There's more in our post on voice and chat.

How much does it cost? It's a handful of costs, not one price. The calls themselves, the phone number, the build, and the care afterward. Real figures, with sources, go in a post of their own. For your own setup, ask us.
