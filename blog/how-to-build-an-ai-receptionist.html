Rabbit hole

How to build an AI receptionist, and how deep it really goes

An AI voice can be talking in an afternoon. Connecting it to your calendar, CRM and team goes far deeper than you probably want to go.

By Kawika LopezOctober 12, 20267 min read

You can get an AI receptionist talking in an afternoon. Getting it to do something useful with each call, like booking the meeting and pulling up the right information, is where it gets deep. That's why it's worth having people who build these every day take it on from the start.

Here's the honest path, so you can see where the work really is before you spend your weekends on it.

Can you build an AI receptionist yourself?

Yes, and the first version comes together fast. Online do-it-yourself tools will give you a voice that answers the phone, greets callers by your business name and holds a simple conversation, often in a single afternoon. That first test call feels like the hard part is done.

It's actually the shallow end. What you have at that point is a receptionist that talks. What saves you time is a receptionist that can look things up while the caller is on the line and does something with the call once it's over.

Getting the conversation right is harder than it looks

A good AI receptionist sounds like your best front desk person on their best day, and getting there takes real customer service know-how. Good customer service isn't easy for people, and teaching it to a voice agent is harder, because every situation has to be thought through ahead of time.

Picture the calls you actually get. The caller who reads their number too fast, the one who's upset because water is coming through the ceiling, and the one who asks a question your website doesn't answer.

Each of those needs a plan for what to ask, what to say, and when to bring in a person.

Even when you get the conversation right, you've only finished the first half.

What it does after the call

A call only pays off when what the caller said ends up where your team can use it. This is where a weekend project turns into a second job.

The meeting needs to be on your calendar. The new lead needs to be in your customer records. Your team might need to hear about specific calls, and an on-call tech might need a text at 2 AM.

Each of those is its own connection between the receptionist and a system you already use, and each one takes technical know-how at a deeper level than building the voice.

Sends to What it does for you Where it gets deep
Your calendar Books the meeting into a real open time Double bookings, reschedules and cancellations
Your customer records, like Salesforce Adds the caller as a new lead with their details Getting each detail into the right place, without duplicates
Slack or email Tells your team about the call right away Who hears about which calls, and when
Text messages Confirms with the caller, or alerts the on-call tech Getting texts delivered to the right person at the right hour

What it looks up during the call

A receptionist that can look things up answers callers like someone who knows your business. That's just as important as what it sends after the call, and it goes just as deep.

Callers ask what times are open this week. They ask when their appointment is, whether their order is ready, or what they still owe. Every one of those answers lives in a system you already use.

Each lookup is its own connection too, reaching into your calendar, your customer records or an account, and each one takes the same deeper know-how to get right.

Looks up What it does for you Where it gets deep
Your calendar Tells callers what's open, or when their appointment is Showing open times without showing anyone else's details
Your customer records, like Salesforce Recognizes returning customers and their history Finding the right person every time, and keeping private notes private
Accounts and orders Answers "Is my order ready?" or "What do I owe?" Giving live answers without giving it more access than it needs
Your business information Answers with your real hours, services and policies Keeping it current as your business changes

Connecting it all together is the biggest job

The biggest piece of all is getting these systems to work together, every time, without anyone watching over them. Your calendar, your customer records, your messages and the receptionist each have their own logins, their own rules for what outside software can see and do, and their own way of writing down a name, a phone number or a time.

The receptionist has to pass information between all of them in the moment, while the caller is still on the line. It has to check the calendar right before it books, find the right customer before it answers, and send the alert only once. When one of those systems changes how it works or goes down for an hour, everything else has to keep running.

That takes real technical comfort, the kind that comes from working deep inside software every day, not just using it. It's the part tutorials skip, and in our experience it's the biggest share of the work.

It also means deciding what the receptionist can see and what it's allowed to change. Can it read your whole calendar, or only the open times? Can it see a customer's full history? Can it move an appointment, or only add one?

Those are the systems your business runs on, and nobody wants to wonder whether a test call just changed a real customer's appointment.

Every business we've worked with has the same story. They tried to build it themselves, went further down the rabbit hole than they ever expected, and eventually stopped and started looking for someone to do it for them. By then they'd spent weeks or months on it.

Setting it up well takes people who do this all the time, involved from the very beginning. Your receptionist should see only what its job needs and change only what it should. Getting that right is our job, not yours.

Have it built by people who do this every day

Having it built for you means you skip the weeks of trial and error and end up with a receptionist shaped around how your business actually runs. Building it in-house makes sense if you have someone on your team, or a whole department, with both the technical know-how and a real feel for customer service. Without that, it's a second job on top of running your business.

When you work with us, you tell us how calls come in and what should happen after each one. The building, the connections, the limits and the upkeep are ours.

We roll it out in stages, starting with a trial run, so you can hear it handle real calls and tell us what you'd like changed. That's less about fixing anything and more about fitting it to you. Once owners hear it working, they think of things they'd like handled differently, like a text instead of an email, or a kind of call they hadn't thought of that should go a certain way. Each stage folds that in, so by the time it's fully live, it fits the way you work.

It books discovery meetings into real open times, and each one goes onto the team's Google Calendar by itself. Questions owners ask

Can I start with a free AI receptionist? A free trial is a good way to hear what an AI voice sounds like on a call. Whether it fits your business comes down to what it connects to and what it's allowed to do, and that's the part worth getting right.

How long does it take to build one yourself? The talking part can take an afternoon. Getting it to do what a business actually needs is where the weeks and months go, and it's where every business we've worked with stopped and went looking for help.

Will connecting it put my systems at risk? Not when its limits are set up well. It should only see and change what its job needs, like the open times on your calendar rather than everything on it. Setting those limits is part of what we do.

What if I've already started building one? What you learned about how you want it to sound is still useful. We build it from the ground up, because the connections to your calendar and records have to be designed in from the start, not added on later. Tell us what you've tried, and we'll show you what we'd build.
